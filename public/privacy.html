Privacy

Last updated 31 October 2026

The Traveling Books keeps what it needs to pass real books between friends: your email, the name and city you give it, and the journeys of the books. Only the people in your groups see your books. Nothing is sold, there are no ads, and you can delete your account whenever you like.

What we keep

What Why
Your email address To send your sign-in links, and only the emails you turn on.
Your name, city and region So friends know who has a book and where it has been.
The books you lend, and each handoff Who sent a copy to whom, when, and the city it was read in. This journey is the point of the app, so it is kept as a permanent log.
Stars and notes you leave about a book Sealed for each reader until they have finished the copy too.
Books on your shelf at home So people in your groups can see what you own and ask to borrow it.
Your groups, friends, and email choices To decide who can see what, and which emails to send.
Errors in the app When something breaks, the error message and the screen it happened on, so it can be fixed. No book contents or notes.

Who can see it

A book, its line, its journey and its notes are visible only to the members of the group it is lent within, and to whoever is holding it or waiting for it. Your name and city are visible to people you share a group with and to your friends. There is no public directory and no search for people. The sample groups are fictional, and real members cannot see each other through them.

These rules are enforced by the database itself, not just hidden on screen, and they are tested on every update.

Photos of your bookshelf

When you scan your shelf, the photo is sent to Google's Gemini service to read the titles on the spines, and the list comes back to you to check. The Traveling Books does not store the photo. Google handles it under the Gemini API terms. If you would rather not, add books by hand instead.

Services we rely on

  • Supabase stores the data and handles sign-in.
  • GitHub Pages serves the website.
  • Gmail sends the sign-in links and any emails you turn on.
  • Google Gemini reads shelf photos, only when you scan.
  • Open Library provides book covers, which your browser loads directly.
  • GoatCounter counts visits to the website without cookies and without tracking you across sites, plus a few anonymous milestones (an invite opened, someone joining, a book lent or passed on) with nothing about who.

On your device

The app keeps your sign-in session and a copy of your groups' books on your device, so it opens quickly. There are no advertising or tracking cookies.

Emails

Apart from sign-in links, every email starts switched off. You choose which ones you want under Profile → Emails, and every email says why you received it.

Deleting your account

Under Profile, Delete my account erases your sign-in and email address, your name, friendships, shelf, group memberships, places in lines and pending emails straight away. Book journeys are shared history, so your stops on them, including on copies you lent, stay as “A reader” with the city, no longer linked to you. Because these are real books, you'll be asked first to pass on any copy you're holding, and to call home and rest any copy of yours that is still travelling.

Encrypted backups are kept for 90 days in case of disaster, so deleted data is gone from them within 90 days.

Children

The Traveling Books is for people aged 13 and over.

Questions

Reply to any email from the app, or open an issue on GitHub. If this policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top will change and members will be told in the app.
